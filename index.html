英文摘要

Acute central nervous system (CNS) injuries, including ischemic stroke, traumatic brain injury, and spinal cord injury, are characterized by profound mitochondrial dysfunction that drives secondary neuronal damage through ATP depletion, oxidative stress, and calcium dysregulation. Neuronal survival is critically dependent on mitochondrial integrity, making bioenergetic failure a central determinant of injury progression. In this context, intercellular mitochondria transfer has emerged as a potential adaptive mechanism through which functional mitochondria are delivered from donor cells-primarily astrocytes, microglia, and mesenchymal stem cells-to metabolically compromised neurons. Mitochondria transfer occurs via both contact-dependent pathways, such as tunneling nanotubes, and contact-independent mechanisms, including extracellular vesicles and free mitochondrial release. These processes are regulated by signaling pathways such as CD38/cADPR-mediated calcium flux and MIRO1-dependent mitochondrial trafficking, facilitating organelle mobilization and uptake. Functionally, transferred mitochondria have been associated with improved oxidative phosphorylation, reduced reactive oxygen species accumulation, altered calcium homeostasis, and modulation of survival pathways including PI3K/Akt and MAPK/ERK signaling in selected experimental models; however, the extent to which these effects are directly dependent on mitochondria transfer remains incompletely established. Preclinical evidence across multiple CNS injury models demonstrates improved neuronal viability, enhanced synaptic function, and attenuation of neurodegenerative processes following mitochondria transfer. However, whether mitochondria transfer represents a primary driver of neurorepair or a secondary compensatory response remains unresolved. In addition, inconsistencies in mechanistic pathways, variability in transfer efficiency, and limited reproducibility across experimental systems hinder translational interpretation. Therapeutic approaches including exogenous mitochondria transplantation, stem cell-mediated delivery, and bioengineered mitochondrial systems show promise but face substantial barriers related to targeting specificity, mitochondrial viability, immunogenicity, and lack of standardized protocols. This review critically evaluates the molecular mechanisms, functional consequences, and therapeutic potential of intercellular mitochondria transfer in CNS injury. It further highlights key limitations and knowledge gaps that must be addressed to determine whether mitochondria transfer can be effectively harnessed as a clinically viable strategy for neuro-restoration.

中文摘要

氧化应激是衰老的标志，也是神经退行性疾病的关键驱动因素。尽管人们对大分子的氧化损伤给予了很多关注，但氧化丰富的三磷酸核苷酸（NTP）池的功能后果仍然很大程度上未知。在这里，我们确定了氧化 GTP (8-oxoGTP)——NTP 氧化的主要产物——不仅仅是损伤标记，而是驱动小胶质细胞激活和神经炎症的内源信号分子。 8-oxoGTP 在衰老小鼠大脑中积累，其升高足以诱发突触功能障碍、tau 蛋白过度磷酸化、神经元损失和认知能力下降。从机制上讲，8-oxoGTP 作为异三聚体 G 蛋白的有效激活剂，与 Gα 亚基的结合比 GTP 本身更强，触发小胶质细胞中的 Src 激酶/MAPK/Akt/NF-κB 信号传导，导致促炎细胞因子的产生、活性氧的产生和神经毒性。 Src 家族激酶的药理学抑制可挽救 8-oxoGTP 诱导的神经炎症和认知表型。我们的研究结果揭示了一种以前未被认识的机制，将核苷酸氧化与年龄相关的神经退行性过程联系起来。

英文摘要

Oxidative stress is a hallmark of aging and a key driver of neurodegenerative diseases. While much attention has been given to oxidative damage of macromolecules, the functional consequences of oxidizing the abundant nucleotide triphosphate (NTP) pool remain largely unknown. Here, we identify oxidized GTP (8-oxoGTP)-a major product of NTP oxidation-not as a mere marker of damage, but as an endogenous signaling molecule that drives microglial activation and neuroinflammation. 8-oxoGTP accumulated in the aging mouse brain, and its elevation was sufficient to induce synaptic dysfunction, tau hyperphosphorylation, neuronal loss, and cognitive decline. Mechanistically, 8-oxoGTP functioned as a potent activator of heterotrimeric G proteins, binding more strongly to Gα subunits than GTP itself, triggering Src kinase/MAPK/Akt/NF-κB signaling in microglia, leading to pro-inflammatory cytokine production, reactive oxygen species generation, and neurotoxicity. Pharmacological inhibition of Src family kinases rescued the neuroinflammatory and cognitive phenotypes induced by 8-oxoGTP. Our findings reveal a previously unrecognized mechanism linking nucleotide oxidation to age-associated neurodegenerative processes.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) serve as critical pathogenic mediators that amplify neuroinflammation, disrupt blood-brain barrier integrity, and exacerbate brain damage after ICH.
