Ginger-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (GELNs) offer multi-target therapeutic potential but are limited by insufficient local retention and rapid clearance. TBI-induced brain-skull adhesion may further aggravate the pathological environment. Overall, CG-GELNs simultaneously improves localized delivery of GELNs, attenuates pathological brain-skull adhesion, and suppresses neuroinflammation, offering a potentially translatable therapeutic platform for remodeling the neuroinflammatory microenvironment and promoting neurological recovery after TBI.

Here, we identify galectin-3 (Gal3) as a regulator of plaque-associated microglial responses, linking Aβ aggregation, lysosomal function, and neuritic pathology. Using postmortem brain tissue from AD patients, we found that Gal3+ microglia preferentially associate with cored plaques and correlate with increased LAMP1+ dystrophic neurites. Gal3 was also detected in microglial lysosomal compartments, extramicroglial dystrophic structures, and in association with extracellular amyloid fibrils, suggesting roles in both intracellular and extracellular Aβ processing. In APP mice, Gal3 deficiency reduced plaque burden and neuritic dystrophy, increased plaque compaction, and enhanced TREM2 coverage around amyloid plaques. Mechanistically, in vitro microglial experiments showed that Gal3 modulates Aβ uptake and lysosomal processing, whereas lysosomal dysfunction promoted the accumulation of fibrillar Aβ from initially monomeric species, particularly in Gal3-deficient cells. Cell-free assays demonstrated that Gal3 preferentially binds fibrillar Aβ, inhibits secondary nucleation, and alters fibril morphology, while Gal3-modified fibrillar Aβ reduces N2a neuronal cell number. Finally, in silico structural analyses identified candidate Gal3 interactors enriched in leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-like proteins, supporting a role for this lectin in immune receptor regulation. Complementary in vivo transcriptomic profiling showed that Gal3 deletion suppresses type I interferon and microglial neurodegenerative transcriptional signatures. Together, these findings position Gal3 at the interface between Aβ remodeling, lysosomal homeostasis, and plaque-associated microglial responses in AD.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO), a key driver of oxidative inflammation released mainly by activated microglia in the neurodegenerative brain, is a potential therapeutic target to inhibit the neuroinflammatory cascade and oxidative damage in AD, but the underlying mechanisms remain unexplored, and achieving targeted brain delivery of an MPO inhibitor and minimizing systemic effects remain significant challenges. Although stem cell-based therapies have shown therapeutic potential, effective control of the inflammatory niche in the diseased brain is critical for improving their therapeutic efficacy. This study investigated whether human nasal turbinate derived neural crest stem cells (hNTSCs) protect dopaminergic neurons by regulating microglia-mediated inflammation through the high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1)-Receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE)-nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway. Here, we identify neuronal interleukin-34 (IL-34) as a critical immunomodulatory signal that maintains microglial homeostasis after cerebral ischemia. We demonstrate that ischemic injury augments IL-34-CSF1R signaling between neurons and microglia.
