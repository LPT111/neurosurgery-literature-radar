神经炎症和小胶质细胞激活是阿尔茨海默病 (AD) 和过量饮酒的常见特征。据报道，酒精会引起肝损伤和炎症，并且 AD 患者血清转氨酶发生改变。小细胞外囊泡 (sEV) 及其负载物正在成为 AD 和酒精性肝损伤中细胞间通讯的介质。然而，饮酒对 AD 进展的影响仍有待阐明。在这项研究中，我们评估了反复酗酒对 APP/PS1 AD 小鼠模型肝脏和大脑的影响。暴露于酒精的 APP/PS1 小鼠血清丙氨酸氨基转移酶水平升高，表明对照组不存在肝损伤，并且肝脏和大脑中促炎细胞因子表达增加。酒精增加了 AD 小鼠和小鼠肝细胞中 sEV 的生物发生和释放。这些 sEV 富含成熟的 miR-122-5p，其在肝细胞中占主导地位。在体内，在酒精暴露的 AD 小鼠的大脑中检测到 miR-122-5p，表明 EV 介导的 miR-122-5p 转移到大脑。体外证实了小胶质细胞对肝细胞来源的 sEV 的摄取。来自酒精处理的肝细胞的 sEV 使 BV-2 小胶质细胞对 LPS 刺激敏感，显着增强 CCL2、CXCL2、CCL3 和 TNF-α 的产生。用 miR-122-5p 模拟物转染小胶质细胞，部分重现了来自酒精暴露肝细胞的 sEV 的促炎致敏作用，而 miR-122-5p 抑制剂递送可逆转这种作用。 ADAM10 是 miR-122-5p 的靶标，在酒精暴露后，野生型和 AD 大脑中的 ADAM10 均减少，并具有细胞特异性效应。这些结果表明，酒精诱导的肝细胞来源的 sEV 可能通过 miR-122-5p 调节肝到脑的信号传导，并导致 AD 中的神经炎症。

英文摘要

Neuroinflammation and microglial activation are common features in Alzheimer's disease (AD) and excessive alcohol consumption. Alcohol induces liver damage and inflammation, and altered serum aminotransferases have been reported in patients with AD. Small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) and their cargo are emerging as mediators of intercellular communication in AD and alcohol-induced liver injury. However, the effects of alcohol use on AD progression remain to be elucidated. In this study, we evaluated the effects of repeated binge drinking on the liver and brain in the APP/PS1 mouse model of AD. Alcohol-exposed APP/PS1 mice showed elevated serum alanine aminotransferase levels, indicating liver injury absent in controls, along with increased pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in both liver and brain. Alcohol increased the biogenesis and release of sEVs in AD mice and in mouse hepatocytes. These sEVs were enriched in mature miR-122-5p that is predominant in hepatocytes. In vivo, miR-122-5p was detected in the brain of alcohol-exposed AD mice suggesting EV-mediated miR-122-5p transfer to the brain. Microglial uptake of hepatocyte-derived sEVs was confirmed in vitro. sEVs from alcohol-treated hepatocytes sensitized BV-2 microglia to LPS stimulation, significantly enhancing CCL2, CXCL2, CCL3, and TNF-α production. Transfection of microglia with a miR-122-5p mimic partially recapitulated the pro-inflammatory sensitization by sEVs from alcohol-exposed hepatocytes, which was reversed by miR-122-5p inhibitor delivery. ADAM10, a miR-122-5p target, was reduced in both wild-type and AD brains after alcohol exposure, with cell-specific effects. These results suggest that alcohol-induced hepatocyte-derived sEVs may modulate liver-to-brain signaling via miR-122-5p and contribute to neuroinflammation in AD.

英文摘要

Huntington's disease (HD) is traditionally regarded as a progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by an expanded CAG repeat in the HTT gene, resulting in selective striatal neuronal degeneration and progressive motor, cognitive, and psychiatric impairment. However, accumulating evidence indicates that HD is a systemic disorder involving profound metabolic, mitochondrial, and immune dysfunction in peripheral organs, particularly the liver. Emerging studies suggest that hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction induced by mutant huntingtin remodels the hepatic secretome through altered secretion of hepatokines, extracellular vesicles, complement proteins, inflammatory cytokines, and metabolic mediators. These circulating factors may disrupt blood-brain barrier integrity, promote microglial immunometabolic reprogramming, enhance complement-mediated synaptic pruning, and amplify chronic neuroinflammation, thereby accelerating neurodegeneration. This review summarizes current evidence supporting the liver-brain axis in HD and discusses how hepatic metabolic dysfunction contributes to central nervous system pathology. Furthermore, we highlight therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring hepatic mitochondrial homeostasis, modulating hepatokines, extracellular vesicles, exosomal microRNAs, and complement signaling to interrupt pathological liver-to-brain communication. Finally, we discuss emerging translational approaches, including multi-omics technologies, engineered extracellular vesicles, gene-editing platforms, and precision medicine, that may facilitate the development of disease-modifying therapies. By integrating peripheral metabolic regulation with central neuroimmune mechanisms, this review presents the hepatic secretome microglia axis as a potential conceptual framework for understanding HD pathogenesis and identifies liver targeted interventions as promising therapeutic opportunities beyond conventional neuron centric strategies.

METHODS: We searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library from inception to November 2025. Randomized controlled trials and observational studies comparing dexmedetomidine versus control in patients undergoing nephrectomy were included. CONCLUSION: Dexmedetomidine provides significant analgesic benefits without reducing clinically defined AKI. Its use as a regional adjuvant warrants further investigation.
