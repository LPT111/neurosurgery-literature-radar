The glymphatic system is a perivascular clearance network that facilitates cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and interstitial fluid (ISF) exchange and removes metabolic waste from the brain. Recent studies suggest that glymphatic dysfunction is not simply a consequence of stroke, but also contributes to disease progression. Impaired glymphatic clearance promotes the accumulation of damage-associated molecular patterns, inflammatory mediators, and toxic metabolites within the brain parenchyma, sustaining both innate and adaptive immune responses. A better understanding of the relationship between glymphatic dysfunction and neuroinflammation may reveal new therapeutic opportunities for improving neurological recovery after stroke.
