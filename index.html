Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is widely used for stem cell mobilization but is associated with side effects such as pain, which often discourages healthy donors. Jing Si Herbal Tea (JSHT), a novel Chinese herbal formula comprising eight different Chinese herbal medicines, has been shown to exert pleiotropic effects against several disorders. The present study primarily explored the potential effects of JSHT aqueous extract on G-CSF-induced stem cell mobilization and the alleviation of G-CSF-induced pain. The effects of JSHT on G-CSF-induced hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) mobilization and pain-related responses in mice were comprehensively evaluated using flow cytometric analysis, nociceptive behavioral assessments, and immunofluorescence staining. Animal studies demonstrated that JSHT had no significant effect on white blood cell (WBC) levels or HSPC mobilization. However, it helped replenish the HSPC level in the bone marrow following G-CSF-induced mobilization of these cells into the peripheral circulation. Furthermore, JSHT increased IFNγ expression in CD45+ and CD45+CD34+ cells, suggesting its potential to enhance HSPC activity. JSHT also alleviated thermal hyperalgesia, with effects comparable to those of acetaminophen (APAP), by reducing neuroinflammation and modulating the expression of cannabinoid receptor 1 (CB1) and the sodium channel Nav1.8 in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG), thereby reducing G-CSF-induced pain. JSHT had no effect on HSPC mobilization into the peripheral blood but might help regulate HSPC level within the bone marrow and potentially enhance HSPC function during G-CSF treatment. Additionally, JSHT effectively attenuated G-CSF-induced pain, suggesting its potential as a supportive therapy during G-CSF-induced stem cell mobilization.

英文摘要

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) causes substantial mortality and disability, with secondary injury extending beyond the initial hematoma. This narrative review examines the working hypothesis that ferroptotic microglia amplify a neurotoxic astrocyte response after ICH. In the iron-rich hemorrhagic milieu, microglial iron loading and impaired antioxidant defenses favor lipid peroxidation, whereas inflammatory astrocyte states marked by LCN2, C3, and MMP3 contribute to barrier dysfunction and neuronal injury. Findings from ICH and related experimental models suggest that oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and inflammatory mediators could connect these responses. Reactive astrocytes may then reinforce myeloid oxidative stress through altered iron handling, glutamate clearance, and complement signaling. Ferroptosis must be considered alongside apoptosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis, and resident microglia share erythrophagocytic and iron-handling functions with infiltrating monocytes/macrophages. These overlapping processes limit the attribution of mixed myeloid signals to a single lineage or death pathway. The proposed bidirectional circuit remains a framework for testing rather than an established sequence in ICH. Therapeutic interception could combine early control of iron-dependent lipid injury with later astrocyte modulation through STAT3 or PPAR-γ, provided that beneficial hematoma clearance is preserved. High-resolution spatiotemporal mapping, lineage-resolved experiments, and sex-specific analyses are needed to identify the relevant cells and treatment windows. Targeting this glial interaction may complement neuroprotection, but its therapeutic value requires direct experimental and clinical validation.
