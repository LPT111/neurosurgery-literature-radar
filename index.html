摘要要点：该内容涉及小胶质细胞、神经炎症、TREM2、炎症。原文：Neuroinflammation is a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease (AD), yet the molecular mechanisms linking microglial dysfunction to amyloid-β (Aβ) pathology remain incompletely understood. Here, we identify galectin-3 (Gal3) as a regulator of plaque-associated microglial responses, linking Aβ aggregation, lysosomal function, and neuritic pathology. Using postmortem brain tissue from AD patients, we found that Gal3+ microglia preferentially associate with cored plaques and correlate with increased LAMP1+ dystrophic neurites. Gal3 was also detected in microglial lysosomal compartments, extramicroglial dystrophic structures, and in association with extracellular amyloid fibrils, suggesting roles in both intracellular and extracellular Aβ processing. In APP mice, Gal3 deficiency reduced plaque burden and neuritic dystrophy, increased plaque compaction, and enhanced TREM2 coverage around amyloid plaques. Mechanistically, in vitro microglial experiments showed that Gal3 modulates Aβ uptake and lysosomal processing, whereas lysosomal dysfunction promoted the accumulation of fibrillar Aβ from initially monomeric species, particularly in Gal3-deficient cells. Cell-free assays demonstrated that Gal3 preferentially binds fibrillar Aβ, inhibits secondary nucleation, and alters fibril morphology, while Gal3-modified fibrillar Aβ reduces N2a neuronal cell number. Finally, in silico structural analyses identified candidate Gal3 interactors enriched in leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-like proteins, supporting a role for this lectin in immune receptor regulation. Complementary in vivo transcriptomic profiling showed that Gal3 deletion suppresses type I interferon and microglial neurodegenerative transcriptional signatures. Together, these findings position Gal3 at the interface between Aβ remodeling, lysosomal homeostasis, and plaque-associated microglial responses in AD.

摘要要点：该内容涉及小胶质细胞、神经炎症、炎症。原文：Chronic neuroinflammation and oxidative stress are important contributors to Alzheimer's disease (AD). Myeloperoxidase (MPO), a key driver of oxidative inflammation released mainly by activated microglia in the neurodegenerative brain, is a potential therapeutic target to inhibit the neuroinflammatory cascade and oxidative damage in AD, but the underlying mechanisms remain unexplored, and achieving targeted brain delivery of an MPO inhibitor and minimizing systemic effects remain significant challenges. We addressed these limitations by first establishing that genetic deletion of MPO in the 5XFAD mouse model improved memory performance and reduced amyloid deposition, validating MPO as a therapeutic target. We then developed a liposome-encapsulated reactive oxygen species (ROS)-activatable MPO inhibitor (LERAMI). LERAMI uniquely combines targeted delivery with on-demand drug release, utilizing a ROS-sensitive linker to selectively release MPO inhibitors in ROS-rich regions within the AD microenvironment, minimizing off-target effects and systemic toxicity. In the 5XFAD mouse model. By reducing MPO expression and activity, LERAMI significantly improved cognitive performance, accompanied by decreased microglial activation, attenuated oxidative DNA damage, improved synaptic and neuronal density, and reduced amyloid burden. RNA sequencing revealed a robust hippocampal transcriptomic reprogramming, with upregulation of pathways critical for synaptic plasticity, calcium signaling, axon guidance, and neuroprotection, along with downregulation of inflammatory and immune response pathways. These findings demonstrate that targeted ROS-activatable MPO inhibition with LERAMI represents a promising therapeutic strategy for AD and potentially other neuroinflammatory diseases, offering a unique approach to mitigate neuroinflammation, preserve synaptic integrity, and restore cognitive function.

Here, we identify neuronal interleukin-34 (IL-34) as a critical immunomodulatory signal that maintains microglial homeostasis after cerebral ischemia. We demonstrate that ischemic injury augments IL-34-CSF1R signaling between neurons and microglia. Neuron-specific overexpression of Il34 (Syn1-Il34tg mice) attenuated infarct volume and neurodeficits after middle cerebral artery occlusion, whereas neuronal Il34 knockdown exacerbated brain injury. Our study reveals neuronal IL-34 as a potential therapeutic target for ischemic stroke.

摘要要点：该内容涉及小胶质细胞、神经炎症、炎症。原文：Diabetic encephalopathy (DE) is a common central nervous system complication of diabetes, characterized by cognitive decline and neuroinflammation. Mitochondrial dysfunction is closely implicated in DE pathogenesis, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, by intersecting published diabetes-associated microglial genes with mitophagy-related genes, we identified SLC15A3, which was highly expressed in microglia. SLC15A3 overexpression induced microglial activation, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation, manifested by increased IBA-1 expression, enhanced migration, elevated ROS and MDA levels, and upregulated pro-inflammatory cytokines. Conversely, SLC15A3 knockdown effectively reversed high glucose (HG)-induced microglial oxidative stress and inflammatory activation. Mechanistically, Nucleotide-binding Oligomerization Domain-containing protein 2 (NOD2) was identified as a candidate interacting protein of SLC15A3. Notably, JNK knockdown significantly rescued SLC15A3-induced mitochondrial dysfunction. Importantly, this SLC15A3-NOD2 regulatory axis was further validated in primary murine microglia. In a microglia-endothelial co-culture blood-brain barrier (BBB) model, SLC15A3 silencing alleviated HG-evoked BBB permeability damage and restored the expression of tight junction proteins. In vivo hippocampal knockdown of SLC15A3 or NOD2 mitigated BBB disruption, reduced neuronal apoptosis, and partially attenuated cognitive deficits in DE mice. Collectively, this study demonstrates that SLC15A3 facilitated mitochondrial lipid peroxidation and mitophagy dysregulation via a NOD2-dependent JNK/DRP1 pathway, thereby accelerating BBB injury and DE progression. SLC15A3 represents a potential therapeutic target for diabetic cognitive complications.

We conducted a narrative review of preclinical and limited clinical evidence on stem cell- and extracellular vesicle/exosome-based therapies for SAH, with emphasis on MSCs and their derivatives. MSC-derived therapies, hold significant promise for mitigating secondary injury and improving outcomes after SAH. Despite this promise, significant translational challenges concerning optimal dosing, delivery methods, and long-term safety must still be addressed. The path to clinical use requires rigorous validation in large animal models before advancing to human trials. Such validation may support the future clinical development of stem cell-based therapies for SAH.

The 1-year rates of target-territory recurrent ischemic stroke were 9.1% with MM and 8.6% with EVT. CONCLUSIONS: Primary adjusted analyses suggested different longitudinal rCBF trajectories between groups, but these differences were not statistically significant after overlap weighting. These findings should be considered exploratory and require confirmation in larger prospective studies.

This study explores the application of machine learning models to predict re-herniation at 12 months using preoperative clinical and demographic data. METHODS: This multi-centered study used data from a randomised controlled trial involving 211 patients who underwent microdiscectomy. After addressing missing data, 152 cases were analysed. RESULTS: Significant predictors of re-herniation included smoking status and emergency department presentations. CONCLUSIONS: Machine learning models, particularly Random Forest, demonstrated moderate predictive ability for re-herniation following microdiscectomy. Significant preoperative factors like smoking and ED visits were strongly associated with poor outcomes. These findings suggest that machine learning can enhance clinical decision-making by identifying high-risk patients, allowing for more personalised treatment strategies.

Enhanced phagocytosis and cytotoxicity of co-activated FcRγ-BAI1/FcRγ-MERTK dual-CAR monocytes drive potent anti-solid tumor activity.

iScience｜2026-12-22

打开 PubMed / 原文

中文摘要

摘要要点：Chimeric antigen receptor macrophages (CAR-M) display superior tumor infiltration compared with CAR-T cells for solid tumor therapy, yet limited antitumor potency and antigen heterogeneity hinder their application. Direct engineering of chimeric antigen receptor monocytes (CAR-Mo) offers a simplified, cost-effective manufacturing alternative. Here, we engineered THP-1-derived monocytic cells with chimeric receptors containing FcRγ fused to phagocytosis-related cytoplasmic domains, targeting nucleolin and HER2. We constructed single-target and dual-antigen CAR-Mo platforms and evaluated their antitumor activity in vitro and in vivo. THP-1 monocytes carrying FcRγ-BAI1 or FcRγ-MERTK receptors exhibited enhanced phagocytosis, and dual-CAR-Mo achieved broader and stronger tumoricidal effects by targeting two tumor antigens. This dual-antigen-targeted CAR-Mo strategy activates both phagocytic and cytotoxic signaling pathways and represents a promising approach for solid tumor treatment. Collectively, this dual-target CAR-Mo strategy demonstrates promising antitumor efficacy in the THP-1 cell model, providing a feasible prototype for future optimization of solid tumor immunotherapy.
