Complementary in vivo transcriptomic profiling showed that Gal3 deletion suppresses type I interferon and microglial neurodegenerative transcriptional signatures. Together, these findings position Gal3 at the interface between Aβ remodeling, lysosomal homeostasis, and plaque-associated microglial responses in AD.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO), a key driver of oxidative inflammation released mainly by activated microglia in the neurodegenerative brain, is a potential therapeutic target to inhibit the neuroinflammatory cascade and oxidative damage in AD, but the underlying mechanisms remain unexplored, and achieving targeted brain delivery of an MPO inhibitor and minimizing systemic effects remain significant challenges. RNA sequencing revealed a robust hippocampal transcriptomic reprogramming, with upregulation of pathways critical for synaptic plasticity, calcium signaling, axon guidance, and neuroprotection, along with downregulation of inflammatory and immune response pathways. These findings demonstrate that targeted ROS-activatable MPO inhibition with LERAMI represents a promising therapeutic strategy for AD and potentially other neuroinflammatory diseases, offering a unique approach to mitigate neuroinflammation, preserve synaptic integrity, and restore cognitive function.

Our study reveals neuronal IL-34 as a potential therapeutic target for ischemic stroke.

Here we investigated whether early-life gluten exposure exacerbates autism-like behaviors in BTBR mice and explored the underlying microbiota-metabolite-brain mechanism. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that early-life gluten exposure induces gut microbiota dysbiosis that drives elaidic acid accumulation, which in turn activates intestinal immunity and suppresses hippocampal immune negative regulation via the gut-brain axis, ultimately aggravating neuroinflammation and autism-like behaviors.

However, the microbial metabolites involved in this process and their functional roles are largely unknown. We screened 352 gut metabolites in induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSC)-derived neurons from individuals with AD to identify compounds that reduce tau hyperphosphorylation. We identified agmatine as a lead candidate, which decreased hyperphosphorylated tau in neurons and cerebral organoids. In 5xFAD mice, agmatine (100 mg/kg/day) improved cognition, reduced amyloid plaques, lowered neuroinflammation, and increased beneficial gut bacteria, including Agathobacter and Bacteroides. Transcriptomic analysis showed that agmatine downregulated complement and inflammatory genes (C1QB, C2, C3AR1, CCL3, CXCL8, and CXCL6) in patient iPSC-derived cerebral organoids. Agmatine also decreased C3aR and pSTAT3 levels in vivo, mechanistically supporting the reduced tau phosphorylation in iPSC-derived neurons. Altogether, these findings identified the therapeutic potential of gut-derived metabolites and identified agmatine as potential treatment for AD.
