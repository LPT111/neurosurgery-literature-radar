Here, we identify galectin-3 (Gal3) as a regulator of plaque-associated microglial responses, linking Aβ aggregation, lysosomal function, and neuritic pathology. Mechanistically, in vitro microglial experiments showed that Gal3 modulates Aβ uptake and lysosomal processing, whereas lysosomal dysfunction promoted the accumulation of fibrillar Aβ from initially monomeric species, particularly in Gal3-deficient cells. Cell-free assays demonstrated that Gal3 preferentially binds fibrillar Aβ, inhibits secondary nucleation, and alters fibril morphology, while Gal3-modified fibrillar Aβ reduces N2a neuronal cell number. Finally, in silico structural analyses identified candidate Gal3 interactors enriched in leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-like proteins, supporting a role for this lectin in immune receptor regulation. Complementary in vivo transcriptomic profiling showed that Gal3 deletion suppresses type I interferon and microglial neurodegenerative transcriptional signatures. Together, these findings position Gal3 at the interface between Aβ remodeling, lysosomal homeostasis, and plaque-associated microglial responses in AD.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO), a key driver of oxidative inflammation released mainly by activated microglia in the neurodegenerative brain, is a potential therapeutic target to inhibit the neuroinflammatory cascade and oxidative damage in AD, but the underlying mechanisms remain unexplored, and achieving targeted brain delivery of an MPO inhibitor and minimizing systemic effects remain significant challenges. We then developed a liposome-encapsulated reactive oxygen species (ROS)-activatable MPO inhibitor (LERAMI). RNA sequencing revealed a robust hippocampal transcriptomic reprogramming, with upregulation of pathways critical for synaptic plasticity, calcium signaling, axon guidance, and neuroprotection, along with downregulation of inflammatory and immune response pathways. These findings demonstrate that targeted ROS-activatable MPO inhibition with LERAMI represents a promising therapeutic strategy for AD and potentially other neuroinflammatory diseases, offering a unique approach to mitigate neuroinflammation, preserve synaptic integrity, and restore cognitive function.

We demonstrate that ischemic injury augments IL-34-CSF1R signaling between neurons and microglia. Our study reveals neuronal IL-34 as a potential therapeutic target for ischemic stroke.

Mitochondrial dysfunction is closely implicated in DE pathogenesis, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, by intersecting published diabetes-associated microglial genes with mitophagy-related genes, we identified SLC15A3, which was highly expressed in microglia. SLC15A3 overexpression induced microglial activation, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation, manifested by increased IBA-1 expression, enhanced migration, elevated ROS and MDA levels, and upregulated pro-inflammatory cytokines. Conversely, SLC15A3 knockdown effectively reversed high glucose (HG)-induced microglial oxidative stress and inflammatory activation. Mechanistically, Nucleotide-binding Oligomerization Domain-containing protein 2 (NOD2) was identified as a candidate interacting protein of SLC15A3. SLC15A3 upregulated NOD2 expression to activate the downstream JNK/DRP1 signaling axis, thereby aggravating mitochondrial lipid peroxidation, reducing mitochondrial membrane potential, and triggering aberrant PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy dysregulation. Notably, JNK knockdown significantly rescued SLC15A3-induced mitochondrial dysfunction. Importantly, this SLC15A3-NOD2 regulatory axis was further validated in primary murine microglia. In a microglia-endothelial co-culture blood-brain barrier (BBB) model, SLC15A3 silencing alleviated HG-evoked BBB permeability damage and restored the expression of tight junction proteins. In vivo hippocampal knockdown of SLC15A3 or NOD2 mitigated BBB disruption, reduced neuronal apoptosis, and partially attenuated cognitive deficits in DE mice. Collectively, this study demonstrates that SLC15A3 facilitated mitochondrial lipid peroxidation and mitophagy dysregulation via a NOD2-dependent JNK/DRP1 pathway, thereby accelerating BBB injury and DE progression. SLC15A3 represents a potential therapeutic target for diabetic cognitive complications.

Conventional surgical and medical interventions have not been sufficient to prevent long-term disability, prompting investigation into novel regenerative strategies. We conducted a narrative review of preclinical and limited clinical evidence on stem cell- and extracellular vesicle/exosome-based therapies for SAH, with emphasis on MSCs and their derivatives. PubMed/MEDLINE and Embase were searched between February and March 2025 for studies examining mechanisms, therapeutic targets, dosing, timing, and routes of administration. Stem cell-based therapies, particularly MSCs and their exosomes, have shown the ability to attenuate neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, and BBB disruption. Key mechanisms include modulation of the HMGB1/TLR4/NF-κB axis, polarization of microglia toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, and secretion of trophic factors such as TSG-6, BDNF, and NT-3. Preclinical studies demonstrate improvements in neurological function, cognitive outcomes, and histological preservation across multiple SAH models. Exosome delivery of microRNAs (e.g., miR-129-5p, miR-193b-3p) has emerged as a potent cell-free strategy. Benefits have been reported after both early and delayed administration; however, differences in experimental models, therapeutic products, dosing methods, routes of administration, and outcome measures prevent direct cross-study comparisons and preclude identification of an optimal treatment window. MSC-derived therapies, hold significant promise for mitigating secondary injury and improving outcomes after SAH. Despite this promise, significant translational challenges concerning optimal dosing, delivery methods, and long-term safety must still be addressed. The path to clinical use requires rigorous validation in large animal models before advancing to human trials. Such validation may support the future clinical development of stem cell-based therapies for SAH.
